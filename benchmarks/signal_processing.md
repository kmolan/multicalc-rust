# Signal-processing accuracy

_Table below generated by `cargo run -p multicalc-qa --bin gen_accuracy_tables`; do not edit it by hand._

Each case is a whole filter chain, not one function call, against
[SciPy](https://scipy.org) `scipy.signal` at a 1 kHz sample rate:

- a gyro chain: notches at a motor frequency and its first two harmonics, into a fourth-order
  low-pass;
- a band-pass that isolates that vibration, so that its frequency can be tracked;
- a high-pass that removes a slow accelerometer bias;
- an encoder curve fit for position, rate and acceleration, at the newest sample and half a window
  behind;
- a range trace with three outliers, through a running median and a moving average.

Each chain has two checks: the design and the magnitude and phase at eleven frequencies from 1 Hz to
450 Hz, then a fixed input sequence through the filter. Thus a design error and a per-sample error
cannot hide each other. The comparisons use tolerances, not bit equality, because the two sides
reach the same filter by different arithmetic.

Three comparisons are narrower:

- At a notch frequency, the phase is not defined, so those points compare magnitude only.
- The median goldens skip the ends, where the reference pads with zeros.
- The curve-fit weights have a looser absolute floor than the rounding noise of the reference.

The moving average is not a `scipy.signal` routine, so it compares against NumPy.
