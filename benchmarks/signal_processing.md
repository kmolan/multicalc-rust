# Signal-processing accuracy

_Table below generated by `cargo run -p multicalc-qa --bin gen_accuracy_tables`; do not edit it by hand._

Each case is a whole filtering job rather than one function call, checked against
[SciPy](https://scipy.org)'s `scipy.signal` at a 1 kHz sampling rate: a drone's gyro chain, where
notches on a motor's frequency and its first two harmonics feed a fourth-order low-pass; a band-pass
isolating that vibration so its frequency can be tracked; a high-pass taking a slow bias out from
under an accelerometer; an encoder read for position, rate, and acceleration at once, both in a live
loop and half a window behind; and a range trace with three bad returns, put through a median and a
mean so the difference between them shows.

Each job is checked twice over — the filter's design and its strength and timing at eleven
frequencies from 1 Hz to 450 Hz, then a fixed input sequence run through it — so a design formula
and the per-sample arithmetic cannot cover for each other. These are tolerance comparisons rather
than bit-for-bit ones: the same filter reached by different arithmetic. Three comparisons are
narrowed for stated reasons — a filter that cancels a frequency has no defined timing there, so
those points compare strength only; the median goldens skip the ends, where the reference pads with
zeros; and the curve fit's weights carry a looser absolute floor than the reference's own rounding
noise. The mean is checked against NumPy, not being a `scipy.signal` routine.
